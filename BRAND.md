# ShareFilesFree — brand & copy guide

Distilled from the marketing/branding reference set in `New Folder With Items 2`
(171 screenshots, mostly @reputeforge carousels). This is the standing guide for
copy and positioning decisions — read it before rewriting any user-facing text.

---

## 1. The core reframe: sell benefits, not features

> "1000 songs in your pocket" beat "7 gigabytes of storage."
> A brand is not about the product, it's about the feeling.
> Sell what *changes*, not what you're holding — the object is just the delivery method.

**Applied here:** nobody wants "WebRTC peer-to-peer transfer." They want *their
4GB video on their friend's phone without making an account.* Every headline
states the outcome; the mechanism is a supporting detail, never the lead.

| Feature (avoid as the lead) | Benefit (lead with this) |
|---|---|
| Peer-to-peer WebRTC | It goes straight there — no upload, no waiting |
| No size cap on P2P | Your 4GB video doesn't need a "Pro" plan |
| Client-side tools | Your files never leave the laptop |
| Files auto-delete | It's gone when you said it should be gone |

## 2. Framing: same fact, better frame

> "80% fat free" outsells "contains 20% fat." Customers buy based on how
> information is *presented*, not just what it is.

State what the user **gets**, not only what we've removed. Pure negation
("no account, no queue, no limits") is our differentiator and worth keeping —
but it should follow a positive promise, never replace it.

## 3. Shrink the downside

> The real objection is fear of getting it wrong. Cut it with guarantees,
> trials, small steps, proof.

For a free, anonymous file tool the actual objections are:
*Is this safe? Will it actually work? What's the catch?*

Answer all three **before** asking for the drop, in plain language. This is why
the "Why it's free" section exists and why it names the one real trade-off
(the link fallback does touch rented storage) instead of hiding it.

## 4. People don't read, they scan

> Most people read in an F/Z pattern. Design for the scan, not the paragraph.

Value must survive on the scan path alone: headline → fact rail → section
headings → CTA. If someone reads only those five things, they should still
understand the whole offer.

## 5. Hooks, pacing, pattern interrupts

- **Hook:** the first line decides if they stay. Lead with tension, a question,
  or a claim worth arguing with.
- **Pacing:** short lines build speed; long ones slow the reader. Vary them
  deliberately — a wall of same-length sentences reads as filler.
- **Pattern interrupt:** repetition makes the eye glaze. Break the rhythm with
  an unexpected line, a shift in tone, or a change of visual weight.

## 6. Small brand vs big brand

> Small brands aim to prove they're **legit and trustworthy**.
> Big brands aim to prove they're **human and they care**.

We're a small brand with zero traffic, so trust is the whole job right now.
Concretely: say exactly where files go, name the trade-offs, don't oversell.

## 7. Anchoring

> The first number people see becomes the reference point for everything after.

Anchor against a **real, checkable** competitor limit (e.g. WeTransfer's free
2GB ceiling), never an invented one.

## Deliberately NOT used

The reference set also covers tactics that would be dishonest here. Recorded so
nobody re-adds them later thinking they were missed:

- **Scarcity / urgency / countdown timers** — there is no scarcity. A fake
  "ends tonight" on a free unlimited tool is a lie.
- **Fabricated social proof** — no review counts, star ratings, testimonials or
  user numbers until they're real and verifiable.
- **Decoy effect / charm pricing / upgrade trap / bundling** — all pricing
  manipulations. There is no price, and adding a fake "Pro" tier to make free
  look better would contradict the entire product promise.
- **The Default Effect** (pre-ticking boxes so people accept them) — we
  deliberately do the opposite: zip-bundling and every privacy option are
  opt-in, unchecked, because the user explicitly asked for that.

The rule: use the psychology that helps someone understand a genuine benefit
faster. Don't use the psychology that manufactures pressure or belief that
isn't earned.
